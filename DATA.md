# The dataset

The brief describes four CSVs in `data/` but the files were not supplied. Zocket
confirmed by email on 9 September 2026:

> Since dataset files are not provided, please generate mock/synthetic data
> following the schema described in the appendix to complete the task.

`data/` is therefore generated by [`tools/generate_data.py`](tools/generate_data.py).
It matches the appendix schema exactly and is deterministic — same seed, byte-identical
output:

```bash
python tools/generate_data.py --out data --seed 20260908
```

2,398 rows of daily ad performance, 8 June to 4 September 2026, 14 campaigns,
32 creatives, 4 channels, INR and USD.

## Why the data is not clean

The brief is explicit about the extract it describes:

> this is shaped like a real extract, and it has the defects real ad data has.
> We did not clean it up for you. Part of the exercise is what you do about that.

Generating tidy data would have removed the exercise. So the generator plants the
defects deliberately, and every one is a mechanism that produces the defect in a real
ad export rather than a random corruption.

### Isn't this circular?

I planted the defects, so of course my agent handles them. Three things keep that
honest:

1. **The generator and the agent share no code.** Nothing in `agent/` imports from
   `tools/generate_data.py`.
2. **`tools/audit_data.py` rediscovers the defects from the CSVs alone.** It was
   written before the generator, against a different synthetic fixture, and has no
   knowledge of what was planted. It currently reports 24 findings; every planted
   defect appears in that list, found independently.
3. **The guards target defect classes, not planted rows.** "Reject a period whose
   final day is below the trailing median" fires on any partial extract. Nothing is
   keyed to a specific row, id or date.

## Defect register

| # | Defect | Rows | Real-world cause | Consequence if unhandled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D01** | `fx_rates` has no weekend rows | 48 days | FX markets close Sat/Sun; rate feeds publish nothing | Inner join drops 2 days in 7 of spend. Totals come out ~28% low, silently |
| **D02** | Final day holds ~37% of usual rows, conversions at ~1% | 11 | Extract pulled mid-day; conversion attribution settles hours to days after the click | Looks like a catastrophic collapse. **This is question 3** |
| **D03** | No rows at all for 19–20 July | 60 missing | Reporting API outage, no backfill | Any daily average over the window is wrong |
| **D04** | 18 fully duplicated rows | 18 | ETL re-run after partial failure, appending instead of replacing | Those rows double-count in every `SUM` |
| **D05** | Negative spend | 6 | Platform credits for invalid/bot traffic, posted against the original day | Legitimate — must not be dropped, but breaks naive `MIN`/ratio logic |
| **D06** | `clicks` > `impressions` | 4 | Bot filtering applied to impressions but not clicks; also cross-device attribution | CTR above 100%. Physically impossible, so a guard should catch it |
| **D07** | Null `revenue` | 23 | Revenue joins from the order system; pending or unmatched orders return empty | Null is not zero. Coercing to zero understates ROAS |
| **D08** | 9 rows reference `C099`, absent from `campaigns.csv` | 9 | Campaign deleted in the platform after performance was exported | Inner join silently discards the spend |
| **D09** | 7 dates written `DD/MM/YYYY`, all with day ≤ 12 | 7 | CSV opened and re-saved in Excel under a different locale | **Parses without error** under MM/DD and lands in the wrong month. Two leave the window entirely; five stay inside on plausible wrong dates |
| **D10** | `channel` values `"Meta "` and `"YouTube"` | 2 | Hand-edited campaign rows | `GROUP BY channel` splits one channel into two buckets, halving reported spend |
| **D11** | 12 rows of C012 (an INR campaign) tagged `USD` | 12 | Campaign currency changed mid-flight; old rows not restated | Converting on the row tag inflates C012 by ~84× |
| **D12** | Duplicate `(2026-08-03, INR)` in `fx_rates` | 1 | Rate feed delivered twice | Join fans out, doubling that day's INR spend |
| **D13** | C005 spend on 2026-08-09 is ~6.5× normal | 3 | Budget cap lifted for a festival flash sale | A **real event, not an error**. Outlier logic must not quietly delete it |

D09 deserves the emphasis. Days 13–31 are safe under a MM/DD parser because month 14
is impossible and any parser falls back to the correct reading. Days 1–12 are the
dangerous ones: `11/07/2026` is 11 July, reads cleanly as 7 November, and nothing
raises. Range checks catch the two that leave the window; the other five are only
caught by validating the raw string format on load, which is what the loader does.

## Ground truth

Derived independently in the audit, and asserted by the regression cases.
Handling applied: dedupe rows, normalise both date formats explicitly, treat
`campaigns.csv` as authoritative for currency, dedupe FX then forward-fill across
weekends, convert every row to USD before aggregating.

**Q1 — spend by channel, 10 July – 4 September (last 8 weeks), USD**

| channel | spend |
|---|---:|
| meta | $51,039.90 |
| google_search | $35,679.09 |
| youtube | $22,587.25 |
| linkedin | $18,403.39 |
| **total** | **$127,709.62** |

**Q2 — most revenue, 1 July – 4 September.** The answer flips on FX handling:

| | winner | value |
|---|---|---:|
| Naive (raw local currency) | C001 Brand Search - India | 2,926,470.88 (INR) |
| **Correct (FX-normalised)** | **C002 Brand Search - US** | **$90,848.14** |

C001 falls to third once converted ($34,921). An agent that sums `spend` without
joining `fx_rates` returns a confident, well-formatted, wrong campaign — and the two
have nearly the same name, so the error does not look like one.

**Q3 — the most recent day.** 4 September holds 11 rows against a 14-day median of 30
(37%), and 1 conversion against a median of 75 (1%). The extract is partial. The
correct answer is that this is a reporting artefact, not a performance collapse.

**Q4 — which campaign to turn off: C007, LinkedIn B2B Leads.** ROAS 0.41 on $18,801
spend across 87 days and 134 conversions — enough history and volume to be sure.
The fixture contains four traps that a ROAS-only ranking gets wrong:

| Campaign | ROAS | Why it must not be the answer |
|---|---:|---|
| C006 YouTube Awareness Q3 | 0.00 | `awareness` objective — never intended to convert |
| C011 LinkedIn Awareness | 0.00 | `awareness` objective |
| C008 Traffic Blast - YouTube | 0.09 | `traffic` objective — judged on cost per click |
| C010 New Product Test | 0.63 | 11 days old, 20 conversions, $410 at stake — not yet decidable |
| C009 Summer Sale - Meta IN | 1.97 | Already ended 15 Aug — cannot turn off what is off |

Eligibility filter: `objective = conversions`, still running, ≥ 30 days of history,
≥ 50 conversions. C007 is the lowest-ROAS campaign that survives it.

**Q5 — competitor spend.** No competitor, benchmark, market or industry column exists
in any of the four files, and no such data is obtainable from ad platforms — it is
their most confidential figure. The correct response is a refusal that names what is
missing and what would supply it, not an estimate.
